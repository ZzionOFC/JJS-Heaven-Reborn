|Chapter 1: Choosing Colors|
This is the most important choice when creating your map: the colors you will use.
Choosing a palette forms the visual foundation of your map. The most common mistake beginners make is overusing hyper-saturated or overly bright tones, which cause visual fatigue.

**The Color Picker Rule:** In most cases, avoid the extremes (the absolute top and bottom) of the color picker.

**The Sweet Spot:** Focus on the middle range of the color picker. This area contains balanced, soft tones that make the environment pleasant for long play sessions.

Notice how the colors in the middle aren't as hard on the eyes? That’s the result you want to achieve.

|Chapter 2: Color Consistency|
**The 3-Tone Rule (60-30-10):** Keep your map visually clean by using a specific ratio for map elements:
**60% Dominant Color:** Floors, main walls, and the map's base.
**30% Secondary Color:** Supporting elements that flesh out the environment, such as roofs, sidewalks, pillars, and interior trim. It should harmonize with the dominant color without clashing with your map's theme.
**10% Accent Color:** Focal points to guide the eye or indicate gameplay elements, such as spawn points, lighting, effects, and small complementary details. Use more vibrant tones here.

|Chapter 3: Layering and Gradients|
**Layering:** This is the technique that prevents the map from looking "flat."
When creating areas where you want to convey depth, use layering: place smaller or larger blocks relative to the main object to add detail and a sense of depth. **Gradient:** Enhances the visual quality of walls and large objects. Use subtle variations of lighter or darker tones in sequential blocks to create gradual lighting, a sense of volume, and visual emphasis.
**Connecting 2 colors:** To create a clean transition between two colors, take the two colors you want to use and blend them by introducing intermediate shades that bridge the gap between them. This improves the visual flow between the colors on your map. (For this, I recommend using the dedicated tool available on our website under the "Colors" tab.)
